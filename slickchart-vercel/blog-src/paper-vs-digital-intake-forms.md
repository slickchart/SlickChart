I used a clipboard for years. It worked. A new client sat down, filled in the front and back, signed at the bottom, and the page went into a folder. Nothing crashed, nobody needed a password, and nobody ever asked me how to download an app.

So this is not a post telling you paper is wrong. Plenty of good estheticians, waxers and PMU artists still run on paper and their clients are safe. It is a look at what paper does well, where it quietly lets a one-person studio down, and what to check before you move anything online.

## What paper genuinely does well

- **Nothing to set up.** A printer and a pen. No logins, no Wi-Fi, no updates on the morning of a full book.
- **Every client can use it.** Some clients do not want to fill in a form on their phone, and that is their call.
- **It is easy to edit.** Cross a line out and initial it.
- **It feels personal.** A well-designed form on good paper says something about your room before the treatment starts.

If your book is small, your regulars rarely change, and you read every form before every visit, paper can serve you fine.

## Where paper lets a solo pro down

The trouble with paper is rarely the form. It is everything around it, and when you work alone there is nobody else to catch it.

**It arrives too late.** A paper intake gets filled in at the start of the appointment. That is the worst moment to learn a client started a retinoid last week, has a sunburn, or is on a medication that changes your plan. You are now rearranging a treatment with the client already on your table and the next person due in an hour.

**It only asks once.** The intake from a client's first visit is a snapshot. Six months later their skin, prescriptions and products may all be different, and the folder does not know. Asking again every visit on paper means another page, another signature and another thing to file.

**A "yes" is easy to miss.** One checked box in a column of twenty looks like every other box. On a busy day, the answer that matters most can sit on page two of a form you skimmed.

**Finding it later is slow.** When a client calls about a reaction, you want their consent, their intake and your notes from that visit in one place, quickly. With paper that means a filing cabinet, and hoping the form was filed under the right name.

**Paper can be lost.** Water, fire, a move, a folder left in the car. When the signed page is gone, so is your proof of what the client agreed to.

## What your state may require

Before you change anything, read your own state board's rules on client records. They differ, and the details matter more than any software.

Oregon is one clear example. Its rule for estheticians (OAR 817-015-0065, which I checked on October 26, 2026) says client records must be kept at the facility for at least three years, "may be stored electronically," and must be "typed or printed in a legible format." Records the state cannot read are treated as incomplete. Your state may say something different, so look up your own board's rules rather than relying on Oregon's.

Whatever the rule, legible and findable are the bar, and handwriting you can read today may not be readable to an inspector years from now.

## What to look for in digital forms

If you do move online, the goal is to fix the problems above, not to swap a clipboard for a tablet that asks the same questions at the same late moment.

- **Clients fill it in before the visit, on their own phone.** That is the whole point. The answers should reach you while there is still time to adjust.
- **Risky answers are flagged.** A checked contraindication should stand out on its own, not hide in a list.
- **It asks again before every visit.** A short check-in (any skin changes, new medications, new products) catches what the first intake never could.
- **Every signature is saved with the date and time,** next to that client's notes and photos, not in a separate app.
- **You can still sign in the room.** For the client who would rather not use their phone, you need a way to hand them your device and have them sign there.
- **The forms look like yours.** Your name or logo, your wording, editable when your policies change.
- **You can print it.** Paper is still the right answer for some clients and some events. A good digital setup includes a printable version.

## The short version

Paper is not unsafe. It is just late, it only asks once, and it depends on you catching everything. If those three things have ever cost you a treatment plan or an afternoon of searching, they are worth fixing, with whatever tool you choose.
