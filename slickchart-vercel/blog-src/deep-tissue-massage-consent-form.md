Deep tissue clients often arrive believing more pressure means better results. A deep tissue consent form resets that before you start, and gives your client the words to tell you when it is too much.

## Describe the work honestly

My form opens with an "about this service" box. Deep tissue is slow, firm pressure into deeper muscle layers, focused on tight or overworked areas. Your client can expect strong but tolerable pressure, worked in gradually, and they undress in private and stay draped.

The same box sets the timeline: some soreness for 1 to 3 days, then easier movement, and regular sessions every 2 to 4 weeks help most. 
Your client then picks today's session length (60, 90 or 120 minutes), any add-ons such as hot towels or aromatherapy, and focus areas: back, neck and shoulders, legs and feet, hips and glutes, or arms. Hips and glutes carry an asterisk tied to a later consent line.

## Put a ceiling on pressure

This is the line I would never leave out of a deep tissue form:

> Deep work should never be sharp or make you hold your breath. On a 1 to 10 scale we stay at about 7 or below, and I'll check in often.

It gives you both a shared number. "That's an 8" tells you exactly what to change.

## The possible effects

The list is plain: muscle soreness for 24 to 72 hours, like after training; feeling lightheaded or tired when they first sit up; brief discomfort while a tight area releases; light bruising, more likely if they bruise easily; a skin reaction to an oil, lotion or essential oil; and redness or tenderness where pressure was firm. Your client confirms they understand these risks.

## A quick health check for deeper work

Twelve items:

- Blood thinners or a bleeding disorder, a blood clot or DVT ever, varicose veins
- Recent surgery or fracture, an injury in the last 72 hours
- High blood pressure or a heart issue, diabetes or numbness
- Pain meds or muscle relaxants today
- Osteoporosis or fragile bones, steroid use (thin skin)
- Pregnant or may be pregnant, and rash, sunburn or open skin

A box asks for more about anything checked. A check is a conversation, not a verdict, and what a health condition means is for their doctor to say. ## The acknowledgements

These lines make the safety rules your client's own:

- They have shared their full health and medication history and will tell you about changes.
- Massage supports wellness and recovery. It does not diagnose, treat or replace medical care.
- They will speak up right away if pressure feels sharp, numb, tingly or more than they can relax into.
- They can change pressure, skip an area or stop at any time, for any reason.
- They stay draped, and glutes, inner thigh, abdomen or chest wall are worked only with their OK first.
- Soreness or light bruising can follow deep work, and results are not guaranteed.
- Intoxication or inappropriate behavior ends the session.

The signature confirms they are 18 or older, or have parent or guardian consent and presence per your policy and local law.

## Aftercare that follows the recovery curve

My deep tissue guide is built around the days after:

- **Today:** sit up slowly, drink water if thirsty, and use ice or heat, whichever feels better. No heavy training or long runs for 24 hours, and no alcohol or hot tubs tonight.
- **24 to 72 hours:** soreness like after a hard workout, maybe light bruising, easing daily. Gentle stretching, walking, a warm bath, or ice for 10 to 15 minutes on sore spots. No pressing or rolling bruised spots.
- **The week:** freer movement. Ease back into training, not straight to the heaviest sessions.
- **Next visit in 2 to 4 weeks:** tell you how long soreness lasted, any bruising, and any new injury or medication. Avoid booking on race day or right after a hard training block.

The guide also suggests planning big events 2 to 3 days after deep work. The call-your-doctor list covers one calf that is swollen, red, warm or painful, numbness or tingling that does not fade, bruising that spreads or keeps growing, and sharp pain or soreness past 3 days. Chest pain or sudden shortness of breath means calling 911, then letting you know so you can note it in their record.

## Questions to decide for your own practice

- Will you chart the pressure number your client named each visit, so the next session starts there?
- Will a client who took muscle relaxants today get a lighter session or a new date?
- What does your state board require? Consent rules and scope of practice vary by state, so check with yours. This post is not legal or medical advice.
