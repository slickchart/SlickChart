Brow waxing is the shortest service on my lash and brow menu, and the one where a skipped question can cost the most. A client who forgot to mention her retinoid can leave with lifted skin right above her eyes. My brow wax and shaping consent is built to catch that before the wax is warm.

## One form for wax, tweeze or thread

The form records the method, then describes the service: unwanted hairs are removed with warm wax, tweezers or a thread to give a clean, balanced shape. It takes about 15 to 30 minutes, mild redness is common and usually settles within a few hours, and results usually last 3 to 5 weeks, depending on hair growth.

Covering all three methods on one form matters, because the client also agrees to a switch if her skin calls for it:

> If my skin isn't suited to wax today, I agree to tweezing or threading instead.

That line lets you change course in the room.

## Risks, with the retinoid rule built in

My list covers redness, tenderness or mild swelling; skin lifting, mostly with retinoids; burns if wax is too warm; bruising, more likely on blood thinners; breakouts or small bumps; ingrown hairs or folliculitis; a shape that isn't quite as expected; and allergy to wax, resin or after-products.

Then the rule, stated in the risk section itself: Accutane in the past 6 months, or a recent prescription retinoid, means no wax, and hairs are tweezed or threaded instead.

## A health check with timeframes

The windows are written into the items themselves:

- Isotretinoin in the past 6 months
- A prescription retinoid on the face in the past 7 days
- Retinol, AHA, BHA or a scrub in the past 5 days
- A peel, laser or microneedling in the past 2 weeks
- Botox near the brows in the past 48 hours
- Eye or eyelid surgery in the past 3 months

It also asks about sunburn or a fresh tan; broken skin, a rash or a cold sore; an eczema, psoriasis or rosacea flare; blood thinners or easy bruising; diabetes or slow healing; allergy to wax, resin or pine; moles or skin tags near the brows; pregnancy or breastfeeding; and new medication or antibiotics.

One acknowledgement ties honesty to outcome: skin lifting and burns are more likely if anything above was left out or changes. The form screens; it doesn't diagnose, and anything that looks medical gets referred out.

My prep guide counts the same timeline down, from 6 months before to the day of the visit, so clients can plan.

## Shape approval before any hair comes off

My form says removed hair takes weeks to grow back, so a shape change can't be undone right away. The client confirms she has seen the mapped shape in a mirror and approves it before anything is removed, and that results and symmetry depend on her natural growth.

My brow design sheet backs this up with measurements for each brow (start, arch, tail, arch height, start width and tail height), a shape goal, and a client approval with a line clients tend to like: brows are sisters, not twins. ## Aftercare by method

The consent gives the headline: no sun, heat, makeup or exfoliation on the area for 24 to 48 hours. The guide fills it in:

- **First 24 hours:** pinkness, mild swelling or tiny bumps are normal. A cool compress or a little aloe gel, hands off, a clean pillowcase, and no makeup on the area for 12 to 24 hours.
- **Days 2 to 3:** cleanse gently and use a light, fragrance-free moisturizer. No sun, tanning, saunas, steam or exfoliation for 48 hours, and no retinol or acids on the area.
- **Weeks 3 to 5:** let strays grow so there is something to shape, and no tweezing at home.

It also explains the difference by method. Wax lifts hairs from the root along with a thin layer of surface skin, so sun and exfoliation wait 48 hours. Tweezing and threading are gentler on the skin, but the follicles are open, so makeup, sweat and fingers stay away for 12 to 24 hours.

Call right away for redness or swelling that gets worse after a day; skin that lifted, blistered or weeps; pus, crusting or growing bumps; skin that feels hot, or a fever; or hives or a spreading rash. Eye pain or a vision change means urgent eye care, and trouble breathing or face or throat swelling means 911. A 3 to 5 week cycle also makes brows a natural rebook; see [rebooking for solo estheticians](/blog/solo-esthetician-rebooking).

## Questions to decide for your own practice

- When a client checks a retinoid, will you tweeze, thread or reschedule?
- What does your state board require? Consent rules and scope of practice vary by state, so check with yours. This post is not legal or medical advice.
