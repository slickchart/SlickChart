Steam feels like a warm towel, so clients rarely think about it. But heat plus lying back is where high blood pressure, a heart condition or fainting matter, and a steamer set too close can scald. A short, clear consent covers both.

## Say what the service is, in one box

My form calls it a scalp facial: a gentle cleanse, warm steam to soften buildup, then a hydrating mask. It takes about 60 to 75 minutes on a padded bed. One line describes the feel and sets the limit at the same time:

> The steam feels warm and misty, never hot.

The same box sets expectations: scalp and hair feel softer for a few days, and visits every 4 weeks help keep things balanced. Then they check today's service: steam scalp facial, hydrating mask, conditioning, scalp massage or a blow-dry.

## Possible risks, named plainly

My list covers what heat and lying back can do: warmth, flushing or brief light-headedness from the heat, temporary redness or tingling, neck or shoulder discomfort from lying back, fresh hair color fading slightly with heat, a burn or scald if steam is too close or too hot, a reaction to the mask, conditioner or fragrance, rarely dizziness when lying back at the bowl, and breakouts along the hairline from rich products.

The box ends with a scope line I would never cut: this is a relaxing cosmetic service that does not diagnose or treat scalp conditions or hair loss, and results vary.

## A health check written for heat

Fifteen items, because heat and lying back touch more than the scalp: high blood pressure, heat sensitivity or fainting, neck or spine problems, sores, rash or sunburn, color or a perm in the last 72 hours, a heart condition, asthma or breathing issues, dizziness or vertigo, psoriasis or dermatitis, Botox or fillers in the last 2 weeks, pregnancy or breastfeeding, migraines or headaches, scalp surgery or a transplant, head lice or a scalp infection, and a fragrance or botanical allergy.

My pre-visit guide says what a check usually means: with high blood pressure or a heart condition, heat and steam are kept gentle, and pregnancy is not an automatic no. Whether a condition rules steam out is for their doctor to say.

## The promises on the signature page

- They have shared their full health history honestly, including blood pressure, heart, pregnancy and allergies.
- They understand this is a cosmetic service, not medical treatment, and results vary.
- They will tell you right away if the steam feels too hot, or they feel dizzy, faint or unwell.
- They will speak up about neck or shoulder discomfort so their position can be adjusted.
- Your specialist does not diagnose scalp conditions and may suggest seeing a dermatologist.
- They will follow aftercare, including no hot tools, saunas or steam rooms for the rest of the day.
- Before and after scalp photos for their private record are OK, with social media use only with their separate OK.

Say the "too hot" promise out loud at the bed. A relaxed client may put up with discomfort to be polite.

## Aftercare for the rest of the day and the week

My steam guide opens with what the service did: warm steam and a hydrating mask leave the scalp softer and hair more conditioned. Then:

- **Today:** the scalp feels soft and a little warm, and light pinkness fades within a few hours. Air-dry or use a cool setting. Skip hot tools, saunas, steam rooms, hot tubs and hard workouts.
- **Days 1 and 2:** hair feels softer, and a few already-loose hairs may shed at the next wash. Gentle shampoo, lukewarm water, a soft brush. No heavy styling products, and no color or chemical services for 48 hours.
- **Days 3 to 7:** the scalp settles back into its usual oil balance. A hat or scalp sunscreen in strong sun, and no very hot showers or scratching with nails.
- **Next visit in 4 weeks:** do not wait too long if the scalp feels tight or itchy.

One good-to-know line is worth repeating: steam softens buildup, it is not a detox.

The call-me list covers burning, blistering or a sore spot, hives or swelling at the hairline or face, a spreading rash, and dizziness or a new headache. For a burn, the guide says to run cool water over it for 20 minutes, then call. A severe headache, facial droop, slurred speech or trouble breathing means calling 911.
