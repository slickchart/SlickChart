A touch-up sounds like the easy appointment. But your client's health may have changed since the session, the skin may not be fully healed, and some spots were never going to hold. A touch-up form checks all of that before the needle goes back in.

## Say what a touch-up is, and when

My "about this service" box describes a short session to fill patchy spots, faded lines or light areas in a fully healed tattoo. It is done once the tattoo is fully healed, no sooner than 4 to 6 weeks. Then it says what is not a touch-up: design changes, additions, and wear from sun, picking or poor aftercare are treated as new work.

The form records the original session date and the placement, so the touch-up sits next to the session it belongs to.

## The risks, for healed skin

- Touched-up areas looking darker for a few weeks
- Scar tissue or blowout that cannot be corrected
- Infection, most often from aftercare problems
- Patchiness that still needs a second pass
- Allergy to ink, even if your client healed well before
- Rare burning or tingling during an MRI scan

A touch-up fills light spots; it does not undo blowout or scar tissue.

## What has changed since the session

Instead of a full health check, this form asks one question: anything changed since your session? Twelve items:

- Pregnant or breastfeeding
- Accutane in the last 6 to 12 months
- Still scabbing or peeling
- A reaction after the last session
- New medications
- A new diagnosis or surgery
- Sunburn or tan on the tattoo
- A rash or cut on the site
- Blood thinners
- Diabetes or immune issues
- Raised or itchy lines
- Keloids or raised scars

"Still scabbing or peeling" catches the client who booked too early. "Raised or itchy lines" and "a reaction after the last session" are worth a careful look; a check starts a conversation, and what a reaction means medically is for their doctor to say.

## What your policy leaves out

A second box names the exclusions on its own, so your client reads them before agreeing: hands, fingers, feet and face; fading from sun, picking, soaking or skipped aftercare; and work by another studio. Your client confirms they understand these, and that some flaws cannot be fixed.

## The acknowledgements

- Your client has told you about any health, medication or skin changes since their last session.
- The tattoo is fully healed, with no scabs, shiny skin or peeling, and at least 4 to 6 weeks have passed.
- Touch-ups follow your studio policy.
- Any change to the design or lettering is new work, which they will approve and spell-check before you start.
- Ink brand, color and lot number used today are logged with the original record for recall tracking.
- They will follow aftercare again for about 1 to 2 weeks and see a doctor if signs of infection appear.

Before and after photos for their record are OK, with social media only by separate release. Your client signs to confirm they are 18 or older with valid photo ID, a copy kept on file, not under the influence, and consenting to this touch-up.

The healed-skin line turns "is it ready?" from a judgment call into something your client has checked and signed.

## Aftercare for a touch-up

My touch-up guide says the fresh work usually heals faster, in about 1 to 2 weeks, but needs the same gentle care.

- **First day:** new spots look darker, shiny and raised. Wrap off at 2 to 5 hours, wash gently with fragrance-free soap, pat dry.
- **Days 2 to 3:** light oozing on the reworked spots only. Thin ointment for 2 to 3 days, then lotion.
- **Days 4 to 10:** small flakes and itching, and new spots may look cloudy. Let flakes fall off. No pools, baths, hot tubs or sun for 1 to 2 weeks.
- **Weeks 2 to 4:** new ink blends into the rest of the tattoo. SPF 30+ once healed, and a photo to you if a spot looks patchy. No more work there for 4 to 6 weeks while deep skin heals.

The call-me list covers spreading redness after day 3, pus, red streaks, fever or lasting bumps: your client sees a doctor, then texts you so you can log the ink batch. Swelling of the face or throat, or trouble breathing, means calling 911.

## Questions to decide for your own practice

- How will you find the original session record, ink and lot when a client books a touch-up?
- What will you say when a spot is blowout or scar tissue rather than a light patch?
- Tattoo licensing and health department rules vary by state and county, so check with your state and local health department. This post is not legal or medical advice.
