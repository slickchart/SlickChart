A makeup artist's best work often happens twice. Once at the trial, and once on the day, months later, when the bride expects to see exactly the face she approved. In between sit a few hundred other faces, a product shelf that has changed, and a phone full of photos with no names on them.

That gap is what a client record is for. Not admin for its own sake, but the thing that lets you recreate a look precisely, catch an allergy before it becomes a problem, and make a party guest feel remembered when they book you again next year.

Here is what is worth keeping, and where to keep it.

## For every client: the basics that protect them

Before any brush touches skin, a few things belong on record:

- **Skin type and concerns.** Dry patches, oiliness, active breakouts, sensitivity.
- **Known allergies and reactions.** Products, ingredients, lash glue, latex sponges. Ask every time, not just the first time. A client who reacted to something new since you last saw them will not always think to mention it.
- **Eye sensitivity and contact lenses.** It changes which products and techniques you reach for.
- **Photo permission.** Whether you may photograph them, and separately, whether you may post the photos. Brides in particular often want their photos kept private until after the wedding, and some forever.

If a client mentions a skin condition or a reaction that worries you, that is a conversation for their doctor or dermatologist, not something to diagnose in the chair. Your record just needs to show what they told you and what you did about it.

## For bridal trials: record the look so you can rebuild it

A trial is only useful if the look survives until the wedding. Most artists who do bridal work regularly end up keeping something like a face chart for each bride. Whatever form yours takes, it should hold:

- **The exact products and shades.** Brand, product and shade name for foundation, concealer, powder, blush, bronzer, brows, eyes, lashes and lips. "Warm nude lip" will not help you in six months. The shade name will.
- **The base and prep.** Skincare and primer used, setting method, anything you did for longevity.
- **Placement and technique.** Where the contour sat, how far the liner extended, which lash style and length, how the brows were shaped.
- **Photos in good light.** Front and both sides, ideally in the same light you will work in on the day.
- **What the bride changed her mind about.** "Softer liner." "Less shimmer on the cheeks." These notes are often the most valuable part of the whole record.
- **What changes before the day.** A spray tan, a new hair color, a skin treatment booked for the week before. Any of these can shift your foundation match.

On the day itself, add how it held up: what needed touching up, what you would do differently next time. That turns one wedding into knowledge you can use for the next one.

## For parties and groups: one record per face

Bridal parties and event groups are where records fall apart, because everything happens fast and everyone is in one booking. Even a short note per person makes the difference:

- Name, shade match and any allergies.
- The look they chose, in a line.
- A photo, so next year's booking does not start from zero.

A bridesmaid who loved her makeup at a wedding is one of the most natural future clients you will ever have. When she books you for her own event, opening her record and seeing her exact foundation shade is the kind of detail people remember.

## For repeat clients: the note that saves the first ten minutes

Regulars come back for a reason: they liked what you did. Your record should let you do it again without re-matching from scratch. Keep their shade matches, their preferred finish, the products they react to, and a line or two of "likes and dislikes." Shades can shift with the seasons or a tan, so note when a match was made, not just what it was.

## Where to keep it

Paper face charts work, and many artists love them. The trouble is everything around the chart: the photos are on your phone, the allergy answer is in a text thread, the shade list is in your notes app, and the bride's booking is somewhere else again.

What helps is having the intake, the allergy questions, the photos and the look notes on the same client record, and having the client fill in their skin and allergy questions before the appointment, not on the morning of a wedding.
