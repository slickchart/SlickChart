Every stylist has met the client who arrives dark brown with a photo of platinum and a free afternoon. Lightening is the service where the hair, not the appointment, decides when you stop. A lightening consent form puts that rule in writing before the foils come out, so stopping early reads as care rather than failure.

This exact form is built into [SlickChart](/slickchart): send it to your client's phone to sign before her appointment, and the lightening aftercare goes home with her.

## Describe the process, not just the result

My form explains that lightener and developer remove natural or artificial pigment, and a toner then sets the shade. It sets the time at about 2 to 5 hours and says that going from dark to blonde often takes more than one session.

The "how long it lasts" line is honest: lightened hair stays light until it is cut, toner fades in 4 to 8 weeks, and regrowth shows by 8 to 12. The client then checks today's service: foil highlights, balayage, global lightening, toner or bond builder.

## Lifting in stages

This is the heart of the form:

> Hair passes through red, orange and yellow as it lightens. Your stylist stops when your hair's condition says so, even before your goal.

Right under it the client records "Strand test done" or "Strand test declined." Later she also says yes to one plain line: big changes, like dark to blonde, may take 2 to 3 or more sessions spaced 4 to 8 weeks apart. Once she has signed both, a brassy first session is the plan working, not a mistake.

## Risks, including the ones blondes dread

The list names scalp irritation, burning or a chemical burn; breakage, dryness or rough texture; over-processing, described in the words clients actually use, "gummy, stretchy or melted" hair; uneven lift, banding or spots; brassy or warm tones that need toning; an allergic reaction to lightener or toner; a goal shade that needs more than one visit; and more porous hair that fades faster.

## Ask about hair history every time

The health and hair check matches my color form on the scalp items (sores, cuts or sunburn, infection, lice or ringworm, psoriasis or eczema flares) and adds what matters most before lightener: past reactions to hair products, a "black henna" tattoo, an allergy to lightener or dye, box dye, henna or metallic dye, and a relaxer, perm or keratin. It also asks about isotretinoin or minoxidil; hair loss, chemo or radiation; pregnancy or breastfeeding; and asthma or sensitivity to fumes.

My pre-visit guide explains why the history matters: box dye, henna or at-home color used in the past 2 years should be mentioned even if it has grown out, because some can react with salon products. A checked box starts a conversation. It is not a diagnosis, and health questions go to the client's physician.

## The acknowledgements

She confirms she shared her full health and hair history, including box dye, henna, relaxers and keratin; that her result is not guaranteed; and that she will speak up right away if her scalp burns, stings or itches. She confirms an allergy alert test for any toner or color, or that she declined and signed the waiver (more in my [hair dye patch test form](/blog/hair-dye-patch-test-form) post). She agrees to follow aftercare, including purple shampoo, deep conditioning and heat protection, and concerns are raised within 7 days.

## Aftercare for thirsty, porous hair

The guide starts with: lightened hair is more porous and thirsty than before.

- **First 72 hours:** hair may feel drier and the tone may look slightly deep at first. Wait 48 to 72 hours before shampooing, and detangle from the ends up with a wide-tooth comb. Skip hot water, swimming, saunas, clarifying shampoo and tight elastics on damp hair.
- **Weeks 1 and 2:** purple or blue shampoo 1 to 2 times a week, plus a weekly deep conditioner and bond-repair treatment. Never leave purple shampoo on longer than the label says.
- **Weeks 4 to 8:** toner fades and brass shows. Book a toner refresh every 4 to 8 weeks, plan the next session if you are lifting in stages, and no home bleach or toner kits.
- **Ongoing:** trim every 8 to 12 weeks; use leave-in, heat protectant and a silk pillowcase; keep hot tools at 300 to 350°F or lower.

The call-me list covers scalp itching, burning or redness; tender spots, blisters or scabs; hair that feels gummy, stretchy or snaps; and a rash or swelling at the hairline, ears or neck. Face, eye or throat swelling or trouble breathing means 911, and severe scalp pain or blistering means medical care that day.

## Questions to decide for your own practice

- At what point will you stop a session, and how will you chart the level reached?
- Where will you note what you used so the next session picks up cleanly? (Here is [what to record in a client chart](/blog/esthetician-client-charting).)
- What does your state board require? Consent rules and scope of practice vary by state, so check with yours. This post is not legal or medical advice.
