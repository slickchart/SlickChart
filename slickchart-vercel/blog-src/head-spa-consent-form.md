Clients book a head spa to switch off, so they need to read the risks before they lie back, not under warm water. A good head spa consent is short, calm and honest about two things: their neck, and what the service does not promise.

## Say what the ritual is, in one box

My ritual consent opens with an "about this service" box. The ritual is a microscope scalp analysis, a double cleanse, massage, a mask and a warm water cascade. It takes about 60 to 90 minutes lying back with the neck supported, and pressure is set to the client and never painful.

The same box sets expectations for afterward: a fresh, light scalp for several days, with many clients rebooking every 2 to 4 weeks. Results vary. Then they check today's service: the full ritual, analysis only, added steam, neck and shoulders, or a blow-dry.

## Possible effects, in plain words

The ritual list is gentle but complete: light tingling or warmth from products or massage, extra shedding of hairs that were already loose, feeling lightheaded when sitting up, water or product reaching the eyes or ears, mild redness for a few hours, a product reaction such as itching, redness or rash, tender neck or scalp muscles for a day, and color fade if hair was colored very recently.

The shedding line earns its place: a client who sees hair in the bowl has already read why.

## Lying back is its own section

This is the part I would never leave out. The form explains that the neck rests on a padded support or a flat head spa bed, never tilted sharply back. If they feel dizzy, see spots or feel numbness, they say so, and you stop and sit them up slowly. A sudden severe headache, slurred speech or face drooping means calling 911.

Then the client makes a choice: extra neck support, or standard is fine. Their answer is on record before the first rinse.

My general service consent adds a short neck note:

> A sharp backward tilt at a shampoo bowl has rarely been linked to stroke. Tell me if you feel dizzy or numb.

## A quick health and scalp check

Twelve items: head lice, ringworm or sores, scalp surgery or a transplant, neck or spine problems, high blood pressure or heart, psoriasis, eczema or dermatitis, color or chemicals in 72 hours, dizziness, vertigo or a past stroke, a fragrance, oil or nut allergy, a sunburned or broken scalp, Botox or fillers in 2 weeks, migraines, and pregnancy or breastfeeding.

A check is where the conversation starts. Whether a scalp condition needs care is for a dermatologist to say, and the form says so: your specialist does not diagnose and may suggest seeing one.

## The general service consent

My head spa service consent covers any service they book. It states plainly that it is relaxing cosmetic care with no diagnosis and no promise of hair regrowth.

Its effects list is wider, adding eye irritation, dryness after cleansing, and hair that feels heavy for a day after oils and masks. It asks clients who are pregnant, epileptic or have a pacemaker to say so.

The client also agrees that no service goes over sores, infection, lice or sunburn, and that the visit may be rescheduled.

## The promises on the signature page

- They will speak up if the water feels too hot, the pressure too firm, or they feel dizzy or unwell.
- Results depend on their scalp, hair and home care, and no hair growth result is promised.
- Blow-drying is offered only if your license allows it where you work.
- Scalp images are part of their private record, with social media use only with their separate OK.

## Aftercare for the days after

My ritual guide follows the days:

- **Today:** slight pinkness or tingling is normal. Sit and stand up slowly. Skip heavy styling products, tight styles and hot tools close to the scalp.
- **Days 1 and 2:** a few extra hairs at the next wash are normal. Gentle shampoo, lukewarm water, no color or chemical services, saunas or scratching.
- **Next visit in 2 to 4 weeks:** rebook before itching or flaking builds up.

The call-me list covers burning or itching that will not settle, blisters or a spreading rash, swelling on the scalp, face or neck, and dizziness or a new headache. Sudden severe headache, slurred speech, face drooping or one-sided weakness means calling 911.
