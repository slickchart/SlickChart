Talk to permanent makeup artists about their software and a pattern comes up. There is a booking app for the calendar and deposits. There is a separate form app, or a stack of PDFs, for consent and the health questionnaire. And the healed photos live in the camera roll, mixed in with everything else on the phone.

Each tool does its job. The trouble is the gaps between them. A PMU appointment is not one visit. It is a consult, a patch test for some clients, the first session, a perfecting session weeks later, and a refresh one or two years after that. The record has to follow the client across all of it, and three separate tools rarely do.

Here is what each job needs, and what to look for in one place.

## 1. Booking that knows a PMU visit is a series

Most booking apps are built for one-off services. PMU is not. The first session and the perfecting session belong together, and the perfecting session has a window. Too soon and the skin has not healed. Too late and you may be doing a full redo.

Whatever you use, check that you can:

- Book the perfecting session before the client leaves the first visit.
- See, on the client's record, when their last session was and what was done.
- Take a deposit in a way your clients already trust.

If your booking and payments already run on Square and work well for you, you may not need to move them at all. More on that below.

## 2. Consent and health forms that arrive before the appointment

PMU consent is not a single signature. A good set covers the health questions that change whether you can work that day, the specific risks of the procedure, the expectation that results vary and a perfecting session is part of the process, and a separate photo release.

The detail that matters most is timing. A health questionnaire filled in on the morning of the appointment is too late to act on. Blood thinners, a recent retinoid or isotretinoin course, pregnancy, a recent cold sore for lip work: you want those answers days ahead, not with the client already in the chair. What each answer means for that client is for their doctor, your training and your state rules to decide, not your software. But your software decides whether you see the answer in time.

Look for forms the client fills out on their own phone before the visit, with risky answers flagged on their own instead of buried in a long PDF. If you want a sense of what a complete PMU set covers, I wrote up the [microblading consent form](/blog/microblading-consent-form) and the [perfecting session form](/blog/pmu-touch-up-form) in detail.

## 3. Photos that tell the healing story

For PMU, photos are not marketing first. They are your record. Before photos show what you started with. Fresh photos show what you did. Healed photos show what the skin did with it, which is the part a client will question months later if anything goes wrong.

The camera roll is a poor place for this. Photos get mixed in with family pictures and are gone if the phone is lost. Ask any tool you are considering:

- Do photos save to the client's record, not my camera roll?
- Are they backed up somewhere other than this phone?
- Can I line up a new photo with the last one, so healed and fresh photos are taken from the same angle?
- Can the client send me a photo at home, if they are worried about a patch at week two?
- Can I keep a photo private, out of anything I post, unless the client signed a release?

## 4. Aftercare the client can find again

PMU healing has stages, and clients panic at the predictable ones: the darkening, the flaking, the patchy look before color settles. A printed card gets lost. Aftercare a client can open again on day five, when the flaking starts, saves you a lot of worried texts.

## Why three subscriptions is the real cost

Each subscription might look reasonable on its own. Added together, the cost is not only the monthly total. It is entering the same client in three places, checking two apps to see whether a form was signed, and searching the camera roll for the right photo when a client asks about their healed result.
