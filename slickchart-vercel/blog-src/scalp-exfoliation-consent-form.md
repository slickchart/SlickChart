Clients ask for scalp exfoliation by feel: heavy, flaky, greasy by day two. It can also leave the scalp a little more sensitive for a day or two. The consent needs to cover timing around color, a patch test for new products, and skin that should never be exfoliated at all.

## Say what the service is, in one box

My form opens with an "about this service" box. Exfoliation is a deep cleanse that lifts flakes, oil and buildup with a scrub, enzyme or clay suited to the client's scalp. It takes about 45 to 75 minutes with gentle pressure and a thorough rinse. One line sets the boundary clearly: tingling is normal, pain is not.

The same box sets the clock. A fresher, clearer scalp lasts about 1 to 2 weeks, and most clients repeat every 2 to 4 weeks. Then they check today's service: fine scrub, enzyme, clay mask, patch test, added massage or added steam.

## Possible effects, without alarm

My list covers what clients actually notice: tingling or warmth while the product works, dryness or tightness for a day or two, stinging if their skin is more sensitive than expected, fine flaking as the scalp settles, redness for a few hours, a product reaction such as itching, rash or swelling, extra shedding of hairs that were already loose, and color fade if their hair was colored recently.

Point out the flaking line at the consult. A client who booked to lose flakes should know brief fine flaking is expected.

## Timing and patch tests get their own box

This is the section that protects the scalp:

> We never exfoliate broken, sunburned or inflamed skin, or within 72 hours of color, lightening, perms, relaxers or keratin.

New products are patch tested first, and the client agrees that their service may be changed or rescheduled if needed. The rule runs both ways: no color or chemical services for 48 hours after exfoliation either, which is in their signed aftercare promise.

My pre-visit guide repeats it: no home scrubs in the 48 hours before, and a check for sunburn or sores.

## A quick health and scalp check

Twelve items, each one changing the plan: head lice, ringworm or sores, a sunburned or peeling scalp, scalp surgery or a transplant, an allergy to fragrance or botanicals, a psoriasis or eczema flare, cuts, scratches or broken skin, retinoid or acne medication, neck problems or dizziness, a seborrheic dermatitis flare, color or chemicals in the last 72 hours, a past reaction to scalp products, and pregnancy or breastfeeding.

A check starts a conversation. What a scalp condition is belongs to a dermatologist to diagnose, and the form says so: your specialist does not diagnose and may suggest seeing one.

## The promises on the signature page

- They have shared their full health, medication and allergy history, including recent color or chemical services.
- Exfoliation is cosmetic care, not a medical treatment.
- They will speak up right away if anything burns, stings strongly or feels too firm, or they feel unwell.
- They agree to a patch test for new products.
- They will follow aftercare, including no color or chemical services for 48 hours.
- Results vary, and no hair growth result is promised.

## Aftercare, day by day

My exfoliation guide opens by saying the scalp may feel a little more sensitive for a day or two. Then:

- **Today:** light tingling, slight redness or warmth for a few hours. Leave the scalp product-free and sit up slowly. Skip heavy styling products, dry shampoo, hot tools and scratching.
- **Days 1 and 2:** a little dryness, tightness or fine flaking. Gentle shampoo, lukewarm water, pat dry. No color, lightening, perms, relaxers or keratin, and no scrubs, saunas or hot tubs.
- **Week 1:** less buildup and cleaner roots. Scalp sunscreen or a hat outdoors, because freshly exfoliated skin burns faster. No home scrubs unless you suggested one.
- **Next visit in 2 to 4 weeks:** do not book color right before the appointment.

One good-to-know note: over-exfoliating irritates, so less is more. The call-me list covers burning or stinging that will not settle, blisters, oozing or crusting, hives or swelling on the scalp, face or neck, and redness that spreads or lasts past 48 hours. Pain, heat, pus or a fever means seeing a doctor, and swelling of the face or throat or trouble breathing means calling 911.
