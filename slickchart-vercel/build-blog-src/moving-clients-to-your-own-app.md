Once your own app works, a new worry arrives. Your clients already know how to book you. They have a link saved, a habit, maybe an app on their phone. What happens to them when you change?

The honest answer is that clients do not migrate. Data migrates. Clients are invited. How that invitation feels decides whether everyone comes with you, and almost all of it is in your control.

## Separate the two moves

Moving to your own app is really two moves that happen to share a week.

**Your records move.** Client names, contact details, allergies, notes, photos, signed forms, appointment history. This is a careful, private job you do on your own, and your clients should never notice it happening.

**Your clients change habits.** A new link to book, maybe a new place to find their aftercare. This is the part they see, and the part to make as small as possible.

Treat them separately. Get the records right first, quietly. Invite clients only once the app already knows who they are.

## Bring the records over before anyone sees a thing

Start by listing everything your current tools hold about clients, and find out how each part can be exported. A client list usually comes out as a spreadsheet. Notes, photos and signed forms vary a lot, so ask your current provider in writing what you can take with you and in what form.

Then decide what your own app needs on day one. It does not have to be everything. A client who opens your app and sees their own name, their upcoming appointment and the aftercare from their last visit feels welcomed. A client who has to fill in their whole history again feels like a stranger.

Two things are worth extra care:

- **Allergies and health answers.** These are the records that keep people safe. Check them by hand on a sample of real clients after the move.
- **Privacy.** Client notes, photos and health answers are sensitive. Moving them into your own app means you are responsible for how they are stored and who can see them, and your app needs a real privacy policy before clients arrive. Check what your state and your insurer expect.

## Make the client's change as small as possible

The best client experience is one where very little changes for them.

- **Keep the old way working for a while.** If you can, let the old booking link keep working, or point it at your new one, rather than leaving clients with a dead end.
- **Do not make installing anything a requirement.** A link that opens in their browser, which they can add to their home screen if they like, asks far less of a client than "download this app first." I wrote about why in [you might not need the app stores](/build/blog/do-you-need-the-app-stores).
- **One link, everywhere.** Your Instagram bio, your website, your text signature, your appointment reminders. Change them all on the same day, so clients only ever see one answer.

## Tell your regulars yourself

A change you explain feels like a step up. A change clients discover on their own feels like a problem.

Tell your regulars directly, in your own voice, before they run into it. A short message is enough: what is new, what stays the same, that their history came with them, and what they need to do (ideally nothing, or one tap). The people who book with you every few weeks are the ones who will notice first and talk about it most, so they deserve to hear it from you.

Then tell everyone else when they next book. The change happens naturally, one appointment at a time.

## Test it as a client before a client does

Before you send a single invitation, be the client. Open the link on your phone as if you had never seen it. Find your own record. Book an appointment. Open the aftercare. Ask a friend who has never used it to do the same, and watch where they hesitate without helping. You do not need to read code for any of this, only the patience to see it through someone else's eyes. I wrote more about that kind of checking in [testing an app you cannot read](/build/blog/testing-an-app-you-cannot-read).

Every place someone pauses is something to fix before your regulars see it.

## Why it is worth the care

The whole point of owning your app is that it fits your clients and your work better than a tool built for everyone. A careful switch is where clients first feel that. Done well, they will not think about software at all. They will just notice that booking you got nicer.
