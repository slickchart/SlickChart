Doing two trades at once: skin and lashes, hair and nails, in one app

November 9, 2026 · 5 min read

Plenty of beauty pros do more than one thing. Skin and lashes. Brows and lashes. Hair and nails. Facials and waxing. Clients love it, because one visit covers two jobs. In my own business, I pair nutrition consults with skin treatments. The treatment comes first, then we talk about what is happening on the inside. Your booking app, on the other hand, often handles it badly.

Look at booking pages from pros who offer two trades and the same patterns come up. Some sell the pairing as a single "combo" service with its own name and price. Some ask clients to book the second service separately, with a note saying it must come straight after the first. Some list one service as an add-on to another. Each is a reasonable workaround. None of them is the booking app understanding that two different trades just met in one appointment.

What actually meets when two services meet

When a client books a facial and a lash lift together, more than two calendar slots come together.

  • Two sets of questions. The facial has its own health and skin questions. The lash lift has its own, plus a patch test rule. Some questions overlap. Some do not.
  • Two sets of rules. The lash lift may need a patch test on file. The facial may have rules about recent treatments or medications. Each rule belongs to its own service, and both have to hold for the combined visit.
  • Two timings, in an order. Which service goes first, how long each takes, and whether anything needs to happen in between is your call as the professional. The calendar should reflect it.
  • Two kinds of aftercare. The client goes home needing both, and should not have to dig through two separate messages to find them.

A combo service hides all of that inside one name. Two linked bookings keep it separate but rely on the client to book them in the right order. Either way, the hard part, knowing which forms and rules belong to which service, still lives in your head.

Rules belong to services, not to visits

Here is the idea that makes this manageable when the app is yours: attach each form and each rule to the service it belongs to, and let the visit collect them.

  • The lash lift carries its patch test rule, its consent form and its aftercare.
  • The facial carries its intake questions, its consent and its aftercare.
  • When a client books both, the visit gathers everything from both services. The client answers each question once, even if both services ask it. They sign each consent once. They get one set of aftercare covering both.

That one decision means you never maintain a "facial plus lash lift" form, a "facial plus brow wax" form and a "lash lift plus brow tint" form. You maintain one set per service, and every combination takes care of itself, including the ones you add next year.

You still decide everything that matters

As with any rule in your own app, the software does not decide what is safe or sensible. You do, from your training, your product instructions, your insurer and your state board. That includes whether two services should be done on the same day at all, which order they go in, and whether anything rules one out when the other is booked. If a combination is never a good idea, that is a rule too, and your app can hold it. For me, there is no pairing I refuse to do on the same day. Your training may tell you differently for yours, and your app should follow your call, not mine.

What the app does is apply your decisions the same way every time, so a busy Saturday does not depend on you remembering which forms a particular pairing needs.

The real work is writing it down clearly

This is a good example of where the effort in building your own app actually goes. AI writes the code, and that part is genuinely solved. What it cannot do is know how your two trades fit together. That comes from you, written plainly:

Each service has its own forms, rules and aftercare. When a client books more than one service in a visit, collect the forms and rules from every service in the visit. Ask each question once, even if two services include it. Show the services in the order I set, with each one's own duration.

Then you think through the awkward cases. What if the client books the second service a week later instead of the same day? What if one service's rule blocks the booking but the other is fine? What does the client see in each case? Answering those is the work. I wrote more about describing features like this in how to describe what you want built, and about rules that should stop a booking in the booking your app should refuse.

Then test it like a client would

Book the facial alone. Book the lash lift alone. Book both together, then book them in the other order. Book them with no patch test on file. Each time, check that the right questions appeared once, the right rules held, and the aftercare covered what was actually done. You do not need to read code to do this, just to know what should happen, and you already do.

Why this matters for anyone with two trades

Doing two trades is one of the clearest ways a beauty business outgrows software built for everyone. Every pairing you offer is a small set of decisions you already make in your head. Writing them down once, and having your own app hold them, is what makes the second trade feel as organized as the first.
