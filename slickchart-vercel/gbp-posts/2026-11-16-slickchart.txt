Switching salon software without losing client history: a checklist for estheticians and beauty pros. New on the SlickChart blog.

The scary part of a switch is not learning a new app. It is the worry that a note, a photo or a signed form gets left behind.

Ask what you are replacing
Most pros are unhappy with one part, not all of it. If your booking works, moving it is the riskiest part. That is how I work: my booking runs on Square and my charting connects to it, so it is all integrated and nothing about booking changed for my clients.

List what your app holds
Contact details, allergies, treatment notes, photos, signed forms with dates, past visits, gift card and package balances, and homecare plans. Every line needs an answer to "how does this come with me?"

Ask what you can export
Ask in writing: client list, notes, photos with names, signed forms as PDFs, and how long you keep access after you cancel.

Check your record rules
Your state board may set how long records must be kept. That does not change when you change apps.

Move the client list first
Open ten clients you know well and check them. Watch for duplicates, and check allergies by hand.

Overlap, then tell clients
Keep the old app open a few weeks. Tell regulars what changes and what stays the same.
