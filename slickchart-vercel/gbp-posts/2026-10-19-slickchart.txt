Waxing software for a one-person studio: what to look for, new on the SlickChart blog.

Most waxing software is sold on booking. But what goes wrong in a waxing room is the client who started a retinoid and forgot to mention it, the reaction nobody wrote down, and the brow client who drifted from four weeks to eight. If you work alone, there is no front desk to catch those things. Your esthetician software has to.

Intake that asks by name
A vague "any medical conditions?" gets a vague no. Ask about isotretinoin, topical retinoids, acids, peels, sunburn and blood thinners by name, and have flagged answers reach you before the client is on the table. Your training and state board set the waiting periods, not your software.

A check before every visit
Medications change between visits. A short pre visit check in, sent every time, catches what a first visit intake form never will.

Client charting about the skin
Which wax, which areas, any lifting, bruising or ingrowns. Treatment notes you can add with gloves on, and see first next time.

Aftercare they can find again
Written aftercare the client can open on day three, when the ingrown question comes up.

Keep what already works
If your booking and payments run on Square, you may not need to replace them. SlickChart adds client charting, consent forms, a pre visit check in and a client app to the Square account you already have.
