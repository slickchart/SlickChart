Makeup artist client records: what to keep for bridal trials, parties and repeat clients. New on the SlickChart blog.

A bride expects the face she approved at her trial, months later, on the day. A client record is what lets you rebuild it exactly.

Allergies, every visit
I learned this one in my own treatment room. A client never mentioned her metal allergy, and she reacted to a treatment. Now I ask about allergies at every visit, metals included, and I write the answer down.

The trial look, in detail
Brand, product and shade name for every step, placement, lash style, and photos in good light. Note what the bride changed her mind about, and anything booked before the day, like a spray tan or new hair color.

One record per face
Bridal parties move fast. A name, shade match, allergies and one photo per person means next year's booking does not start from zero.

Remember the person, not just the shade
I write down big life events, like a wedding, a new baby or a move, so I can ask about them next time. Clients tell me my memory is incredible. Really it is the note, and what they feel is that I care.

Keep it in one place
SlickChart is the client record I built for this: intake and allergy forms clients fill in on their phone ahead of time, photos saved to the client record, and notes for every look. It works with the Square account you already book through.
