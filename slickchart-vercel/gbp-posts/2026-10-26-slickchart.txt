Paper or digital intake forms for a solo esthetician? An honest look, new on the SlickChart blog.

I used a clipboard for years and it worked. Paper needs no setup, every client can use it, and it feels personal. If your book is small, paper can serve you fine.

Where paper lets a solo pro down
It arrives too late. A paper intake form is filled in at the start of the appointment, the worst moment to learn a client started a retinoid or has a sunburn. It only asks once, so the first visit snapshot goes stale. One checked box in a column of twenty is easy to miss. And when a client calls about a reaction, finding their consent forms and treatment notes means a filing cabinet.

Check your state board
Rules on client records differ by state. Read your own board's rules first. Legible and findable is the bar.

What to look for in digital consent forms
Clients fill them in on their own phone before the visit. Risky answers are flagged for you. A short check in goes out before every visit. Every signature is saved with the date, next to your client charting and photos. You can still have a client sign on your device in the room, and you can still print.

How I handle it now
SlickChart is the esthetician software I built for my own room. Intake and consent forms reach the client's phone first, and each signed form sits on the client record. It works alongside the Square account you already book through.
