Esthetician courses and guides: turn the advice you repeat every week into something you own. New on the SlickChart blog.

How many times this week did you explain how to start a retinol, or what not to do after a peel? Each of those is a lesson you give live, for free, one client at a time.

Guides versus courses
A guide is one topic on one page, like aftercare or a three-step routine. Most are free. A course is a series with an outcome, like a barrier reset or a pre-wedding skin plan. That is where a price makes sense.

What to make first
Start with what you already explain most. The course my own clients love most is my 6-week vitality reset.

What goes inside
One idea per lesson, short enough to read on a phone. Name the products you recommend and make each one easy to buy from inside the lesson. Attach a routine chart or checklist. Stay in cosmetic scope and say when to see a doctor.

Pricing
There is no standard price. Think about what the client would otherwise spend to get the same guidance. Some include a course with a package and sell it alone to clients who are not local.

Delivering it
Course platforms suit creators with big audiences. You have a client list. You want to write it once and send it in a tap, somewhere your client already looks.
