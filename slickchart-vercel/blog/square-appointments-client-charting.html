Square Appointments and client charting: what it does, what it doesn't, and how to fill the gap

September 21, 2026 · 4 min read

A lot of solo estheticians land on Square for sensible reasons. It books clients, it takes payment, it sends reminders, the card reader is cheap, and you were probably already using it before you thought of yourself as a business. None of that is a mistake, and this post isn't going to tell you to rip it out.

What I want to do instead is separate two things that get muddled together: a client profile and a client chart. They sound similar. They do completely different jobs, and the difference only becomes obvious the day you need the second one.

A profile is who they are. A chart is what happened.

A profile holds contact details, appointment history, what they've spent, maybe a notes box. That's genuinely useful — it's the "who" of your business.

A chart is the "what." What you did on 4 March, at what strength, how the skin looked before you started, what it looked like fourteen days later, what they reacted to last spring, what you agreed to try next. It's per-visit, it's cumulative, and it's the thing you reach for when a client says "my skin went funny after the last one" and you need to know exactly what the last one was.

Most booking software is built to fill a diary. Charting is a different problem, and it usually shows.

The five-minute audit

Rather than take my word for what any booking system stores — including Square, which changes what it offers regularly enough that anything I tell you today could be out of date by the time you read it — go and look at your own account. Five minutes, and you'll know exactly where you stand.

Open a real client you've seen several times, and answer these:

  1. Can I see visit-by-visit notes, or one notes box? If it's one box, ask yourself what it'll look like after thirty visits. A single field is a scratchpad, not a record.
  2. Where are their before-and-after photos? If the honest answer is "my camera roll," they aren't attached to anything. Try finding March's photos for a specific client and time yourself.
  3. Is their signed consent form on this screen? Or is it attached to one appointment, or in a different app entirely, or in a folder?
  4. If a client asked what I used on them in April, how long would it take? Under a minute is a record. Ten minutes of scrolling isn't.
  5. Can I get all of this out? Notes and photos, in bulk, in a format you can open — not one client at a time.

Whatever software you're on, those five answers are the real picture. Feature lists won't tell you; your own account will.

The three-places problem

Here's the pattern I see constantly, and it isn't anyone's fault — it's what happens when you solve each problem as it arrives.

Booking and payment live in one place. Consent and intake forms end up in a separate forms app, because you needed something clients could sign before they arrived and your booking system didn't do it. Photos live in your camera roll, because that's where the camera puts them.

Three places. The client's history is spread across all three, and none of them knows about the other two. That's not a tidiness complaint. It means that when you actually need the record — a reaction, a dispute, a client returning after two years, an insurer asking a question — you're reconstructing it from three sources under pressure.

It also usually means you're paying two subscriptions to still not have a chart.

Two honest ways to close the gap

Replace everything with an all-in-one. There are good ones, and if your booking setup genuinely isn't working for you, a clean break can be the right call. Be realistic about the cost: migrating clients, rebuilding your services and hours, retraining yourself in your own diary, and telling regulars the booking link changed. That's the reason most people who say they're going to switch never do.

Or keep what works and add what's missing. If booking and payments are fine, the thing you're actually short of is the chart. You don't have to tear out a working system to get one.

That second route is why SlickChart connects to your own Square account rather than replacing it. Your booking, your payments and your card reader stay exactly where they are; notes, photos, consent forms and aftercare sit on top, attached to the client and the visit. Nothing to migrate, and no new booking link to explain to anyone.

Before you pay for anything

Ask the same four questions of any option, including mine:

  • What does this cost once forms and everything else I need are switched on?
  • What does migration actually involve — an import, or retyping my clients?
  • Can I export my notes and my photos, in bulk, if I leave?
  • Who at the company can read my client notes?

Skin notes plus contact details is sensitive information, and "we take security seriously" isn't an answer to the last one.
