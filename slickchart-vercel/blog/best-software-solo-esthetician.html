Best software for a solo esthetician in 2026: an honest category breakdown

October 12, 2026 · 5 min read

Every "best software" roundup I've read has the same flaw. It ranks products that aren't trying to do the same job — a booking platform and a clinical records system end up on the same list, one of them wins, and you still have no idea whether the winner solves your problem.

So instead of a ranking, here are the four kinds of software a solo esthetician actually chooses between, what each is genuinely built for, and what it costs. I checked every price below on the company's own pricing page on 12 October 2026. Pricing in this category changes constantly and plans get renamed, so check it yourself before you pay anyone.

1. The all-in-one booking platforms

Built around the calendar: booking site, reminders, payments, marketing, and a client record hanging off the appointment.

Vagaro lists one location at $30 a month, with $23.99 for the first six months, plus $10 for each bookable calendar up to seven. Notably, its pricing page says SOAP notes are included with every plan. For a solo pro who wants structured visit notes on a small budget, that's a genuinely strong offer, and I'd rather say so than pretend otherwise. Forms are listed as a feature but aren't priced on the page, so ask what that costs.

GlossGenius lists Standard at $28 a month and Gold at $56 ($24 and $48 billed annually), with forms and waivers starting on Gold, not Standard. Processing is a flat 2.6%. It's the most polished booking experience in the category for a one-person business, and that's not nothing when you're using it forty times a week.

The free-to-start platforms are a real option too. If one is free, the only question that matters is how it earns instead: a subscription later, your processing rate, or a fee on each new client it sends you. Get that number per booking before you commit, because it's the part that grows as you do.

2. The clinical platforms

Built around the chart: photo markup, structured treatment records, consent, storage, sign-off.

This is where it gets expensive, and it's worth understanding why. GlossGenius's medspa line starts at $168 a month ($148 annually) for Practice Essentials, which is where its EMR tools, charting, photo markup and 50 GB of photo storage live. Practice Advanced is $298 ($248). The dedicated aesthetics EMRs sit in the same world.

These are real products built for real clinics, and the price reflects a clinic rather than a single treatment room. If you're working under a medical director, this category is where you belong and the cost is part of the job. If you're doing facials and waxing on your own, you're being quoted for a building you don't have.

3. Priced per seat, charting included

Mangomint lists $120 a month per location plus $10 per user, with forms and charting included rather than sold as a tier. Processing is 2.45% + 15c in person and 2.90% + 30c virtual.

Charting at the base price is the right instinct and I wish more of the category copied it. But a solo pro pays the same $120 location fee as a six-chair salon, so per person it's the most expensive option here — and it's openly built for teams, which is a perfectly reasonable thing to be.

4. Keep your booking, add the chart

Then there's the option most roundups leave out, because it isn't a platform at all.

If your booking and payments already work — for a lot of us that's Square — then what you're actually short of is the chart. Square Appointments has a tier aimed at solo professionals and it does the diary and the money well. What it gives you per client is a profile: contact details, appointment history, a notes box. That isn't the same thing as a per-visit record with photos, strengths, reactions and the aftercare you sent. I wrote about why a profile and a chart are different jobs separately.

That gap is the whole reason SlickChart exists. It connects to the Square account you already have instead of replacing it, so your calendar, your booking link and your payments stay exactly where they are and your clients never see a change. You add the chart that was missing.

The limits, plainly: it isn't a booking system, so if booking is your broken part this won't fix it, and it needs Square. And if you're on Vagaro and happy, you already have SOAP notes. Stay where you are.

How to actually decide

One question, and it isn't about features.

Is your booking and payment working? Not perfect. Working — clients book without texting you, money lands, reminders go out, you don't dread opening it.

  • Working, and the notes are the gap → add charting. Don't tear out the half that's fine. What switching really costs you is mostly invisible until you're in the middle of it.
  • Not working → you have two problems, and one platform that does both well is probably right. But decide it on the booking. Decide on what's broken, not on what's missing.
  • Medical oversight involved → category two, and don't try to save money there.

Four questions before you pay anyone

  1. What does it cost with everything I actually need switched on? Forms, texts, marketing. The arithmetic is here.
  2. What's the card-not-present rate? It's usually higher than the headline rate, and deposits and card-on-file charges are card-not-present.
  3. If I leave in two years, what do I get back, and what does the export cost? Ask for a price in writing. The review sites are full of people who found out afterwards.
  4. How long am I required to keep these records? Your state board sets that, not a software company. Ask them, not a sales rep.

There is no best. There's the one that fixes your real problem with the least disruption — and you already know which half of your setup you dread opening.
