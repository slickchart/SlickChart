Switching beauty software without losing your client history: a checklist

November 16, 2026 · 4 min read

The scariest part of changing software is not learning the new app. It is the quiet worry that something will not come with you: the notes from a client's first visit, the photo that shows how a reaction healed, the consent form a client signed two years ago.

That worry is reasonable. Moving software is the moment records get lost, and a lost record is the one you will need later. Here is a checklist for doing it carefully, whichever apps you are moving between. It also covers one question worth asking first: do you need to move everything at all?

Step 1: Decide what you are actually replacing

Most pros who start looking for new software are unhappy with one part of it, not all of it. Maybe the booking side is fine but the charting is thin. Maybe the forms are clumsy but your clients love how they book.

If your booking and payments work, moving them is the riskiest, most disruptive part of any switch. Clients have to learn a new booking link, your history of past appointments may not come along, and every regular notices. It is worth asking whether you can keep the booking you have and add what is missing. That is how I work. My booking runs on Square and my charting connects to it, so everything is integrated and nothing about booking changed for my clients. I wrote more about that in do you have to switch to get charting?

If you do need to replace everything, carry on.

Step 2: Make a list of everything your current app holds

Open your current software and write down every kind of record in it. For most solo beauty pros, that list looks something like:

  • Client contact details: names, phone numbers, emails, birthdays.
  • Allergies and health answers: the things that keep clients safe.
  • Treatment notes and charts: what you did, with what products, and how the skin responded.
  • Photos: before and after, progress, reactions.
  • Signed forms: intake, consent, photo releases, policies, with their dates.
  • Appointment history: past visits and services.
  • Money owed in either direction: gift card balances, package sessions left, deposits held, store credit.
  • Product recommendations and homecare plans.

This list is your checklist. Every line needs an answer to "how does this come with me?"

Step 3: Ask your current provider exactly what you can export

Do not assume. Ask, in writing, and keep the answer. Useful questions:

  • Can I export my client list as a CSV or spreadsheet? Which fields come with it?
  • Can I export treatment notes, and in what format?
  • Can I download client photos, and will I know which client each one belongs to?
  • Can I download signed forms as PDFs, with their signing dates?
  • Will I keep read-only access to my account for a while after I cancel?
  • How long do you keep my data after I leave, and can I ask you to delete it?

Some answers will be "yes, easily." Some will be "not really." Better to find out now than the week after you cancel.

Step 4: Check how long you have to keep records

Your state board may set rules on how long client records must be kept and in what form. Those rules do not stop applying because you changed apps. Look up your own board's requirements, and make sure that anything that cannot be moved is still kept somewhere you can reach and read for as long as you need it. An exported PDF folder you can open is better than an account you no longer pay for.

Step 5: Move the client list first, then everything else

Your client list is the backbone. Get it into the new app, check it, and only then layer on notes, photos and forms.

  • Spot-check, do not skim. Open ten real clients you know well and confirm their details came across correctly.
  • Watch for duplicates. The same client entered twice with a different email is common, and easy to clean up now and painful later.
  • Check allergies specifically. It is the field that matters most if it goes missing.

Step 6: Run both for a short overlap

Keep the old app available, even read-only, while you settle into the new one. The first few weeks are when you will think "where is that note?" and want to look it up. Cancel only when you are confident nothing you need is left behind.

Step 7: Tell your clients what changes for them

If your booking link, forms or reminder messages are changing, tell your regulars before they notice. A short message is enough: what is new, what stays the same, and that their information came with them.
