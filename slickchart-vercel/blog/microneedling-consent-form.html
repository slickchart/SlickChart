Microneedling consent form: screening, expectations and aftercare for a cosmetic-depth treatment

October 19, 2026 · 5 min read

Microneedling is the one treatment on my menu where the skin is genuinely open for a day or two. Tiny channels let products sink in far deeper than usual. Everything about the consent form and the aftercare follows from that one fact.

This exact form is built into SlickChart: send it to your client's phone to sign before the visit, and the aftercare follows them home.

Before anything else, a word on scope. Whether an esthetician may offer microneedling at all, with what device and to what depth, is decided state by state. Check with your state board before you add it to your menu or adapt any form, and treat this post as practical guidance, not legal or medical advice.

Explain the treatment and the device

My form describes the treatment in one breath: an FDA-cleared pen with a sterile, single-use needle cartridge makes tiny channels at a cosmetic depth to improve texture and tone. Then what the client will feel (light pressure, vibration or a sandpaper feel, with bony areas more tender) and an invitation to ask for a break.

Expectations come next: redness like a sunburn for 1 to 3 days, then dryness, with results building slowly over a series and not permanent. Other options include no treatment, peels or homecare, or a physician for deeper scars, laser or prescription care. Naming the physician route keeps you inside your scope.

Put hygiene and depth in writing

Clients worry about needles they cannot see being opened. My form answers it with an acknowledgement the client initials:

A new sealed, sterile cartridge is opened for me each visit. Depth stays within cosmetic limits.

It is a promise and a boundary at once, and a written record of the standard you work to.

Risks that are specific to needling

The risk list has its own items: redness, swelling, pinpoint bleeding and bruising; darker or lighter patches, more likely in deeper skin tones; dryness, peeling, itching, milia or breakouts; a cold sore flare for anyone who has had them; infection, or a reaction to products or metal; and track-like marks or scarring, which are rare.

A contraindication list built for open skin

The checklist is stricter than a facial's because the barrier is opened on purpose. Mine asks about:

  • Pregnancy or nursing
  • Accutane (isotretinoin) in the past 6 months
  • Retinoid, acids or scrubs in the past 5 days
  • Active acne, cold sore, warts or rash in the area, and eczema, psoriasis or rosacea there
  • Sunburn now, or tanning in the past 2 weeks
  • Blood thinners or a bleeding disorder
  • Diabetes, a weak immune system or chemotherapy
  • Keloids or raised scarring
  • Allergy to numbing cream, metals or nickel
  • Botox, filler, peel or laser in the past 2 weeks
  • Being under 22 years old

The nickel item pairs with the metal reaction risk. The checklist screens; it does not diagnose. A yes is a reason to pause and, where needed, ask for the client's doctor's input.

The rest of the form is short: health history is complete and true and changes will be shared before each visit, results vary and a series of 3 to 6 visits is typical, aftercare and daily SPF 30+ will be followed, the client can stop at any time, and there are no refunds once performed. Add photo permission choices and a parent or guardian line.

Aftercare: clean and gentle is the whole plan

The first night is the strictest part, and it surprises clients most:

  • Today: red, warm and tight like a sunburn, with mild swelling or a little clear fluid. Clean hands only. Tonight, rinse with lukewarm water and apply only the soothing product you gave them. No makeup, no sunscreen, no touching, sweating, alcohol-based or scented products.
  • Days 1 and 2: gentle cleanser, plain moisturiser, mineral SPF 30+, a clean pillowcase. Mineral makeup only after the time you gave. No workouts, sauna, pools, the beach or direct sun.
  • Days 3 to 7: dryness and light flaking. Let flakes fall. No retinol, acids, vitamin C, scrubs, waxing or picking for 7 days.
  • Week 2 onward: restart the routine slowly once nothing stings, rebook 4 to 6 weeks out, and no tanning or other facial treatments until you have checked the skin.

My guide explains why: strong actives, fragrance or anything not sterile can sting, irritate or trigger a reaction while the channels are open, so use only what you have approved.

Close with when to call: pus or spreading bumps, redness, heat or pain getting worse after day 2, fever or swollen glands, a cold sore, lines or dark patches that do not fade, or any sign of allergic reaction. Trouble breathing or swelling of the face or throat means calling 911.

Questions to decide for your own practice

  • Does your state board allow microneedling for estheticians, and at what depth and with what device?
  • Which serums are approved for the first night, and are they named in your aftercare?
  • How will you record device, depth and passes for each visit? (What to record in a client chart covers this.)
