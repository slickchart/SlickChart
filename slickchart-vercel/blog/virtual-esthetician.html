Virtual esthetics: how a solo esthetician earns outside the treatment room

November 11, 2026 · 4 min read

Every solo esthetician hits the same ceiling. There are only so many facials one pair of hands can do in a week, and once the book is full, the only lever left is price. I walked through that arithmetic in the math behind a six-figure esthetician. The short version: the room has a limit.

Virtual esthetics is the part of the work that does not need the room. Not a second business, and not a reel strategy. It is the advice you already give away every week, in DMs, in texts, at the front desk, turned into something a client can book, read or buy.

That is why I started doing virtual consults myself. I wanted to grow my income without adding more service hours, and to stop only trading my time for money.

There are three ways to do it, and most estheticians already do the hard part of all three without being paid for it.

1. Virtual consults

A virtual consult is a skin conversation that happens before, between or instead of a visit. It comes in two shapes.

  • A skin analysis (photo) consult. The client sends guided photos of their skin from their own phone and answers your questions: what they use now, what bothers them, what they have tried. You look, you write back with a plan and product picks.
  • A product consult. No photos. "What should I be using?", "I ran out of my serum, what now?", "my skin changed this winter". You review their routine and send picks.

Who books these? A new client who found you online and wants to know you before committing to a facial. A regular between visits. Someone who moved away and still trusts you. Someone three states away who will never sit in your chair at all.

Two things to keep straight. First, a virtual consult is cosmetic advice, not a diagnosis. Anything that looks medical goes to a doctor or dermatologist, and your notes should show you said so. Second, licensing rules vary by state, and some boards have views on advice given across state lines. Check with your own state board before you market consults nationally. I go deeper on both in virtual skin consultations.

2. Guides and courses

You explain the same things over and over. How to use a retinol without wrecking your barrier. What to do the week after a peel. How to build a morning routine that takes three minutes. Each explanation is a small lesson.

Write it once, well, and it becomes a guide you send in a tap instead of a speech you give ten times a week. Group a few into a series and it becomes a course: a 30-day acne routine, a pre-wedding skin plan, a barrier repair reset. Free guides build trust. Paid courses are a product. Both keep working when you are not. My clients' favorite is my 6-week vitality reset course. More on what to make first in selling skincare guides and courses.

3. Shoppable homecare plans

Most product recommendations die on the drive home. The client nods, means to buy the cleanser, forgets the name, and buys whatever is on the shelf at the drugstore.

A shoppable plan fixes that. Every product you recommend, from a facial, a consult or a guide, lands in the client's hands with a way to buy it right there: from your shelf at their next visit, from your online shop, from the brand through your affiliate link, or from Amazon under your own tag. They pick the way that suits them, and the sale is yours instead of a stranger's. My own virtual clients buy only through my affiliate links, which means I never hold inventory for them. I cover the setup, and the disclosure rules that come with affiliate links, in shoppable homecare plans.

How the three connect

The reason to do all three is that they feed each other.

  1. A stranger finds your consult link in your Instagram bio and books a photo consult.
  2. Your reply includes a plan and three shoppable picks. They buy two.
  3. You send a guide on how to introduce the new serum, with those products linked inside it.
  4. A few weeks later you follow up for a photo in the same light. If they are local, that is when they book a facial.

None of that adds a single hour in the treatment room. It does take some time at a desk, so price it like the expertise it is.

What it takes in practice

You can stitch this together from a DM inbox, a Google Form, a PDF tool and a list of links. Plenty of people start that way. The trouble is that the photos, the advice, the products and the follow up end up in five places, and none of it is attached to the client's record.

That is why I built these into SlickChart: photo and product consults with a public consult link, a guide and course builder, and homecare plans where every pick is shoppable in office, through your shop, your vendor affiliate links or Amazon. It all sits on the client's record next to their charts, and SlickChart never takes a cut of what you sell. It connects to the Square account you already use.

Whatever you use, start small. One consult type, one guide, one product list. The skill you are selling is the one you already have.
