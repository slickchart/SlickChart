Powder and ombré brows consent form: style, saturation and healing

October 19, 2026 · 5 min read

Powder brows are often where a client lands after you have told her strokes will blur in her skin. That makes the consent form for shading a little different from a microblading form. It needs to name the style she chose, set expectations for a softer and more solid look, and be honest about the one risk that is specific to shading: too much of it.

This exact form is built into SlickChart: send it to your client's phone to sign before the visit, and the aftercare below goes home with her.

Name the style before anything else

The first question after her name is a single choice: powder, ombré or combo. That one answer frames everything after it, and it lives in her chart for the touch-up.

The "about this service" box then explains the method in a sentence: a single-use machine needle builds soft, shaded color, and combo adds hair strokes up front. It sets the appointment at about 2 to 3 hours, says a touch-up at 6 to 10 weeks is part of the process, and notes that this technique suits oily and mature skin.

How long it lasts, and why combo differs

My form says powder brows usually last 2 to 5 years, depending on skin, sun and aftercare. Then a detail that heads off a question a year later: combo strokes fade sooner than shading. If she chose combo and the front strokes soften first, she was told.

The risk that is specific to shading

Most of the risk list matches my other brow forms: uneven fading or asymmetry refined at touch-up, allergy to pigment or numbing even months later, rare granulomas, color change as it fades, scarring if skin is damaged, infection without good aftercare, and a Koebner reaction for psoriasis or vitiligo. The MRI note is there too: tell the radiologist and never skip an MRI because of PMU.

The first item is the one that belongs to this service alone:

A heavy or block-like look if over-saturated

Putting that in writing tells her a soft first result is deliberate, and gives you a reason to build density at the touch-up rather than chase it in one session.

The health check

The quick health check is the same list I use for microblading, with one difference worth noticing: this form asks about Accutane in the past 6 months, where my microblading form asks about the past 12. The rest carry their windows too, such as retinol or acids on the brows in 14 days and Botox in the past 2 weeks.

Anything she checks gets a free text box. That is a starting point for a conversation, not a verdict, and deciding what a health condition means is her physician's job, not ours.

Design approval covers density, not just shape

Her approval line on this form names the shape, the style, the density and the pigment color, all checked in a mirror before you begin. Density is the word that matters here. A client who approved "soft" has a reference point when she wonders why it is not darker.

My brow prep guide helps that consult go well: come with brows unwaxed and untweezed for 1 to 2 weeks so you can see her natural shape, bring the brow pencil or powder she loves, and decide on microblading or powder together based on her skin.

Acknowledgements

She confirms an honest health history, that results vary with skin type, age, sun and healing, and that her brows will look dark and bold, then flaky and patchy, before color returns at 4 to 6 weeks. She agrees a touch-up at 6 to 10 weeks is usually needed to even out color and density, that this is semi-permanent and removal is not guaranteed, that private before and after photos are required, and that deposits and refunds follow your policy.

Aftercare for thicker flakes

My powder brow guide tracks the same stages as microblading, with the differences called out. Days 1 to 3, brows look darker and more solid than they will heal. Days 4 to 10, flakes are thicker, and picking can leave bare patches. Days 10 to 21 is the lighter stage. By weeks 6 to 10 the result is soft and powdered, lighter than day one, and she comes back to perfect density and color.

The quick rules: hands off the flakes, a rice-grain of balm, no swimming, sauna or steam for 14 days, and no tanning for 4 weeks, then SPF daily.

Questions to decide for your own practice

  • Which clients do you steer to powder, and which to combo?
  • How much density will you build in the first session versus the touch-up?
  • How will you remind her about the touch-up window? (See rebooking for a solo practice.)
  • PMU licensing, health department and bloodborne pathogen rules vary by state and county, so check with your state and local health department and board. This post is not legal or medical advice.
