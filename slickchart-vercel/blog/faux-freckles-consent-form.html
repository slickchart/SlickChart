Faux freckles consent form: the freckle map, fading and aftercare

October 19, 2026 · 5 min read

Faux freckles look like the easiest service on your menu: small dots, one session, a quick heal. That is exactly why the consent form matters. A client expecting every freckle to stay put, exactly where it was placed, is a client who will be disappointed at week three. Her form needs to say, kindly and clearly, that some freckles will fade away and that this is normal.

This exact form is built into SlickChart: send it to your client's phone to sign before the visit, and the aftercare below goes home with her.

Describe it, and offer the alternatives

My form explains the service in one line: tiny dots of light brown pigment placed with a machine or by hand to look like natural freckles. Then it covers the plan: one session, then a touch-up in 6 to 8 weeks, and freckles that soften and fade over about 1 to 3 years.

It describes what she will feel, quick pricks or a light scratching feel, and says numbing may be used as your state allows. And it lists her other options: no treatment, freckle pens or makeup, or temporary freckle tattoos. For a service this cosmetic, naming the low-commitment alternatives is part of real consent.

Name the result she might not expect

The risk list starts with the most likely surprise:

Some freckles heal lighter or disappear (normal)

The rest: placement or spacing that looks uneven to her, an allergic reaction to pigment or numbing products, freckles that spread, blur or heal too dark, a color shift over time, and infection or darker marks in deeper skin tones.

"Uneven to you" is a thoughtful phrase. Natural freckles are not evenly spaced, and the form acknowledges that how they look is partly a matter of her eye, which is why the map approval below matters.

A health check that keeps the door open

The heading on her checklist sets the tone: a yes does not always mean no. You may wait, adjust the plan, or ask for her physician's OK. That framing keeps clients honest, because checking a box does not automatically cost them the appointment.

The items carry their windows: Accutane in the past 6 months, retinol, retinoids or acids on the area in 14 days, a peel, laser, IPL or microneedling in 4 weeks, sunburn, tanning or self-tanner in 2 weeks, and chemo or radiation in the past 6 months. It also asks about pregnancy or breastfeeding, acne, rash, a cold sore or broken skin in the area, keloids, blood thinners, a bleeding disorder or diabetes, and allergies. A free text box follows. A check is a conversation, not a diagnosis.

The freckle map and photo choices

Before you start, she approves her freckle map and shade. That map is your shared reference at the touch-up, when you are filling in the ones that faded rather than redesigning.

Photo permissions are split. Before and after photos for her private chart are a separate yes or no. Social media or website use is optional, and she chooses face hidden or face visible. She can withdraw photo permission at any time, in writing.

Acknowledgements

She confirms she has shared her health history and skincare honestly and will tell you about any change before her touch-up. She accepts that some freckles may heal lighter or fade away completely, and that the touch-up helps even them out. She agrees to follow aftercare, not pick scabs, and use SPF once healed, and accepts that results are not guaranteed. She can stop at any time, and services cannot be refunded once performed.

Aftercare for a small, fresh tattoo

My guide opens with a reminder that freckles heal fast but are still a fresh tattoo.

  • Day 0: freckles look darker and more defined than they will heal. Blot with clean tissue and wash hands before touching her face. No makeup on the area, sweating, heat or steam.
  • Days 1 to 4: freckles feel slightly raised and tiny scabs form. Rinse gently, pat dry and use a rice-grain of balm. No rubbing, scrubbing or face cloths.
  • Days 5 to 7: the tiny scabs flake off and freckles may look lighter or patchy. Let them come away on their own.
  • Days 7 to 14: makeup on the area from day 14, or when you say it is OK.
  • Weeks 3 to 8: final color settles, and some may heal lighter or disappear. Daily SPF 30 or higher once healed, and come to the touch-up.
  • Long term: SPF every day, keep retinoids and acids off the area, and tell laser or IPL providers about the freckle tattoo.

The call-me list and a 911 line for trouble breathing or swelling of the face or throat close it out.

Questions to decide for your own practice

  • How will you save her freckle map so the touch-up matches? (See what to record in a client chart.)
  • Will you suggest a freckle pen trial before booking a first-time client?
